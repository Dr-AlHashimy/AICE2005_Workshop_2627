# AICE2005 Integrated Design Assignment

## Designing and Vibe-Coding a Note-Taking Application

## Overview

Across the workshop series, your team designs a note-taking application from
first principles — personas, stakeholders, requirements, UML design,
prioritisation, and a test plan — then uses that specification to **vibe
code** a working implementation with an AI coding assistant. The assignment
brings these two halves together: a **specification document** built from
your workshop artefacts, and a **working application** built from that
specification, defended in a **group presentation with individual oral
questioning**.

## Learning Outcomes

By completing this assignment, you will demonstrate that you can:

- Identify personas and connect user needs to application behaviour (W1).
- Specify functional and non-functional requirements traceable to personas
  and use cases (W2).
- Map stakeholders by power and interest, and show how they influence the
  system (W3).
- Design the system's structure and behaviour using UML class and sequence
  diagrams in standard notation — visibility, types, multiplicity,
  relationships and packages (W4).
- Prioritise your requirements using MoSCoW, with each priority justified
  against personas, stakeholders or use cases (W5).
- Plan how your requirements will be tested, with a test case for each
  requirement (W6).
- Use a specification to direct an AI-assisted ("vibe coded")
  implementation, and explain where the implementation does — or doesn't —
  match what was specified.
- Individually explain and defend your team's design and implementation
  decisions under direct questioning.

## Deliverables

Each group (up to five members) submits two components: a **specification
document** and a **working application**, presented together in a **group
demo with individual oral questioning**.

### 1) Specification Document (PDF)

Compiled from the artefacts produced across the workshops. Carry each
template's content into the final document rather than submitting the raw
templates:

| Section | Source workshop |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Personas & Use Case Diagram | W1 |
| 2. Functional & Non-Functional Requirements | W2 |
| 3. Stakeholder Mapping | W3 |
| 4. UML Design — Class & Sequence Diagrams | W4 |
| 5. Prioritisation — MoSCoW | W5 |
| 6. Test Plan | W6 |

A team logo (W0) may be used as a cover page, optional.

### 2) Vibe-Coded Application

A working note-taking application, built by directing an AI coding
assistant using your specification. Submit a link to the repository (with
commit history visible) alongside the specification document.

### 3) Group Presentation & Individual Oral Q&A

A short group demo of the working application against your specification,
followed by individual questioning — each team member is asked questions
directly about the design and implementation, to assess their individual
understanding of and contribution to the work.

Recommended structure:

- Slide 1: Introduce your app and your team.
- Slide 2: Personas & the problem you're solving (W1).
- Slide 3: Requirements, stakeholders, MoSCoW priorities & how they shaped your design (W2, W3, W5).
- Slide 4: System design — UML class & sequence diagrams (W4).
- Slide 5: What you built vs. what you specified — and why they diverge,
  if they do.
- Live demo of the application.
- Individual oral questions (no slide — asked directly during/after the demo).

| Criterion | Weight | Excellent | Good | Satisfactory | Poor |

Individual oral Q&A is scored per student, independently of the group marks
above — it is how each team member's individual understanding and
contribution are assessed.
